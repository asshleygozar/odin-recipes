Spaghetti Recipe

Description

Spaghetti is a long, thin, solid pasta made from durum wheat or semolina flour and water. It's a staple of Italian cuisine and is often served with tomato sauce, meatballs, or vegetables.

The word spaghetti comes from the Italian word spaghetto, which is a diminutive of spago meaning "thin string" or "twine"

Ingredients

  • spaghetti
  • tomato sauce
  • garlic
  • onion
  • olive oil

Steps

  1. Fill a large pot with plenty of cold water (use 3 quarts of water for every 4 to 8 ounces of dried spaghetti). If desired, add salt to the water for seasoning. Bring the water to a rolling boil.
  2. Once the water is boiling, add the spaghetti a little at a time so the water doesn’t stop boiling. Reduce the heat slightly so the water doesn’t boil over; boil, uncovered, until the spaghetti is al dente, using the package directions as a guide for timing.
  3. To bring out spaghetti’s full, nutty flavor, cook it until it has the firm slightly chewy texture known as al dente (Italian for “to the tooth”). Test near the end of cooking time by giving it a taste.
  4. When the spaghetti is cooked al dente, drain it in a colander and shake well to remove excess water that could make your sauce runny.
